1. Introduction 
Agriculture is the main livelihood for many people in the North Central Province (Rajarata). Even 
though our farmers produce a large harvest, they face many problems when trying to sell their crops. 
We noticed that there is a big gap between the farmers in villages and the buyers in big cities. 
Because of this gap, farmers often don't get a fair price, and a lot of vegetables go to waste before 
they even reach the market.[1] 
 
Our project, "HarvestHub," is a software solution designed to solve this problem. It is a digital 
platform that connects farmers directly with buyers Instead of relying on  middlemen, our system 
creates a "Bidirectional Marketplace" where farmers can list what they have, and buyers can post 
what they need.  
 
The main motivation for this project is to fix the "Trust Gap." Usually, buyers in places like 
Colombo are afraid to buy from remote farmers because they cannot check the quality of the 
vegetables. To fix this, our system uses Agricultural Officers to manu ally check and approve the 
quality of the harvest before it is sold. We also included a way to manage transport costs 
automatically so that farmers don't have to worry about finding vehicles.  
 
2. Problem Statement 
Our team has analyzed the current agricultural system in the North Central Province (Rajarata) and 
identified three main problems that our software, HarvestHub, will address: 
2.1. Information Asymmetry (Farmers Don't Know Demand) 
Currently, farmers harvest their crops without knowing the real demand in the market. They often 
grow the same crops as everyone else at the same time, leading to an oversupply. This causes prices 
to crash and results in farmers dumping their vegetables because they cannot find buyers.[2] 
2.2. The Trust Gap (Buyers Can't Verify Quality) 
Buyers in distant cities (like Colombo) are afraid to buy directly from small scale farmers because 
they cannot verify the quality of the vegetables without seeing them. Because there is no one to 
guarantee the quality, buyers prefer to use middlemen, which reduces the profit for the farmer. 
2.3. Logistical Inefficiency (Transport is Hard to Find) 
For small scale farmers, finding a vehicle to transport a small harvest is very difficult and expensive. 
Often, there is no standard price for transport, and the delay in finding a vehicle causes the fresh 
produce to spoil before it reaches the market.[3]

2.4. Financial Risk & Payment Uncertainty 
In the current system, there is no secure way to handle payments. Farmers are afraid to send goods 
without money, and buyers are afraid to pay without receiving goods. There is no central system to 
manage advance payments or ensure fair transactions between strangers. 
3. Proposed Solution 
HarvestHub is a complete digital ecosystem designed to solve traditional agricultural supply chain 
inefficiencies. It connects four key users in one unified platform: Farmers, Buyers, Agricultural 
Officers, and Transporters. 
Novelty Compared to Existing Marketplaces 
Unlike existing agricultural platforms that only act as simple online classifieds or notice boards 
(leaving users to independently figure out quality trust, transport, and delivery), HarvestHub 
introduces three key features: 
• Built-In Logistics Coordination:  Transport is automatically assigned according to harvest  
capacity, rather than forcing buyers to hire independent trucks. 
• Verified Quality Assurance: Instead of unverified seller claims, mandatory visual evidence 
(photos/videos) must be certified by official Department of Agriculture officers before a listing 
goes live. 
• End-to-End Supply Chain Tracking: Real-time order and transport tracking from the farm 
gate directly to the buyer's doorstep. 
3.1. For Farmers 
Farmers download the mobile app, register with their personal details and exact farm GPS location, 
and create listings for their harvest. The app guides them to capture clear photos and a short video 
of their freshly harvested crops. Once submitted, the li sting undergoes official quality verification 
before going live on the marketplace. Farmers can also browse "Wanted" ads from buyers and 
respond to direct demand. 
3.2. For Buyers 
Buyers (such as shop owners, supermarkets, or wholesalers) use the app to search for available 
crops. They can filter listings by produce type, verified quality grade (A, B, or C),  They can also 
post specific supply requests (e.g., "Need 200kg of Grade A tomatoes by Friday" ). Once a buyer 
selects a listing, they can negotiate via in-app chat, finalize the purchase, and immediately arrange 
integrated transport.

3.3. For Agricultural Officers 
Officers from the Department of Agriculture access a secure desktop dashboard to review pending 
crop submissions from local farmer s. Only approved and graded listings are published on the 
marketplace to guarantee buyer trust. 
3.3.1. Quality Review Method 
To ensure fair and standardized grading without needing to travel to every farm 
immediately, officers follow a systematic, digital-first evaluation process: 
1. Media Authenticity Check: The system verifies the timestamp and GPS geotag of the 
uploaded photos and videos to ensure the media was captured recently at the registered 
farm location. 
2. Visual Inspection: Officers examine the images and video footage to assess key visual 
parameters: color uniformity, size consistency, surface blemishes, and signs of pest 
or fungal damage. 
3. Standardized Grading: Based on Department of Agriculture guidelines, the officer 
assigns a grade: 
o Grade A (Export/Supermarket Quality):  Uniform size and color, zero 
visible damage or decay, optimal freshness. 
o Grade B (Standard Retail Quality): Slight size variation or minor superficial 
skin blemishes permitted; no rot or structural damage. 
o Grade C (Processing/Bulk Quality):  Safe for consumption and food 
processing (e.g., sauces, jams) but lacks visual uniformity or has noticeable 
aesthetic defects. 
3.4. For Transporters 
Vehicle owners and independent drivers register on the app to view available delivery jobs. They 
can accept requests that align with their geographic route and vehicle weight capacity. The system 
automatically calculates a fair, transparent delivery fee ba sed on exact travel distance and cargo 
weight, reducing transit times and post-harvest spoilage. 
4. Scope of the project 
The HarvestHub project is a digital solution designed to fix the current gaps in the agricultural supply 
chain in the North Central Province. The scope covers building a "Trust Ecosystem" that connects 
farmers, buyers, and transporters through a verified and managed platform.

5. Aim and Objectives 
5.1. Aim 
The aim of this project is to develop a trusted agricultural marketplace and logistics ecosystem 
that connects farmers directly with buyers, ensuring fair trade through officer -verified quality 
checks, automated transport cost calculation, and a secure payment-first transaction workflow.  
 
5.2. Objectives 
The project will be achieved through the following six specific and measurable objectives:  
1. To develop a GPS-Verified Crop Listing Module: To build an interface for farmers to view 
current market demand and create crop supply listings using real-time, location-tagged photos 
to ensure authenticity. 
2. To implement an Officer Verification Dashboard:  To build a centralized desktop portal 
for Agricultural Officers to manually review, grade (e.g., Grade A), and approve crop photos 
and GPS data before they are published to buyers. 
3. To create a Marketplace Negotiation System:  To develop an application module that 
enables direct communication between farmers and buyers to negotiate prices and securely 
schedule pickup dates. 
4. To build an Automated Logistics and Dispatch Engine:  To implement a system that 
automatically calculates transport costs based on weight and distance, assigns suitable vehicles, 
processes advance payments, and dispatches drivers. 
5. To build a Pre -Loading Quality Verification Feature:  To create a checkpoint in the 
Transporter App where the transport helper inspects the crops and uploads photos, allowing the 
buyer to remotely approve or reject the harvest quality before the advance payment is released 
to the farmer. 
6. To implement a 2-Way Final Delivery Confirmation: To build a final step where only the 
transporter (confirming drop -off) and the buyer (confirming receipt) verify the delivery, 
automatically completing the order and releasing final payments without requiring any manual 
confirmation from the farmer.

6.5. Database Design  
The system utilizes a cloud -based NoSQL document database to ensure scalability, real -time 
synchronization, and flexible data structures. The data is organized into the following primary 
collections: 
• Users Collection: Stores user documents including unique ObjectId, user role (Farmer, Buyer, 
Driver, Officer), contact information, and dynamically updated trust rating scores used to 
evaluate reliability. 
• Listings Collection: Stores farmer crop submissions as documents. This includes the unique 
ObjectId, a reference to the farmer, embedded geospatial data (GPS coordinates), media links 
for the Smart Camera photos, available quantity, price, and the current verification status (e.g., 
Pending, Under Review, Approved). 
• Market Requests Collection:  Stores buyer demand documents, containing the buyer 
reference, required crop type, requested quantity, and target location. 
• Orders Collection: Acts as the central transaction record. It stores the current state -machine 
status (e.g., Awaiting Payment, In Transit, Completed), advance payment confirmations, and 2-
way completion signatures. 
• Logistics & Tracking Collection:  Stores transportation metadata linked to specific orders, 
including calculated travel distance in kilometers, total transport cost, and embedded location 
data for route tracking. 
• Chat Collection:  Efficiently stores real -time messaging documents between buyers and 
farmers for negotiation and pickup scheduling.

6.7. Collaborative Quality Verification Queue 
• Implemented using JavaFX · Workflow:

• Status = PENDING (Visible in a global queue to all Agricultural Officers)      
• Available Officer claims the listing from the queue      
• Status changes to UNDER_REVIEW (Listing is temporarily locked to this officer to

• Officer reviews the GPS data and Smart Camera photos

listings become visible in the marketplace to buyers

• Fetch farmer and buyer GPS coordinates

matching a 1-ton lorry to an 800kg harvest)    
• Calculate final dynamic transport fee using the multi-factor pricing formula (Distance + Weight 
+ Fuel rate) · Final transport details are stored as a document in the Logistics & Tracking 
Collection (MongoDB)

6.11. Project Methodology – Incremental

We have chosen the incremental model for our project. Below are some of the main reasons for 
choosing it.  
1. Early Delivery of the Marketplace  
While we are still developing complex  logistics and payment algorithms, farmers can start 
registering their farms and “officially -verifying” their crops. This builds  a logistics database 
early in the project lifecycle.  
2. Complexity Management of Multi-Role Interactions  
Trying to build all these interfaces at once is very complex. With this incremental model we 
have:  
• First, stabilize the Farmer/Officer relationship (Increment 1).  
• Then, introduce the Buyer negotiation (Increment 2).  
• Finally, integrate the Driver/Helper workflow (Increment 3)  
3. Better Handling of Physical Verification

This is a technical challenge. By reserving this for a later increment, we ensure that the "smart 
guidance capture" in Increment 1 has already been perfected and produces high -quality geo-
tagged data for the driver to use during the verification phase. 
6.12. Business & Revenue Model 
To ensure long -term financial sustainability while driving massive user adoption, HarvestHub 
employs a "Split Transaction Fee" model. The system acts as an automated accountant, securely 
processing all payments and strictly deducting service commissions only upon successful 
deliveries. This ensures platform goals are perfectly aligned with user satisfaction. 
• Buyer Convenience Fee (3%): Applied to the Buyer’s total order value at checkout. This fee 
monetizes the convenience of bypassing traditional middleman markups, saving travel time, 
and securing verified quality crops. 
• Transporter Commission (5%):  Deducted automatically from the final delivery fee. 
HarvestHub acts as a guaranteed lead generator for logistics providers, eliminating empty return 
trips and idle stand time, which easily justifies the commission. 
• Farmer Fee (0%):  To heavily incentivize agricultural sector adoption and rapidly build the 
platform’s supply side, Farmers are charged zero platform fees for listing or selling their 
harvests. 
• Refund Policy (Zero -Fee on Failure):  If a transaction results in a rejection or dispute, the 
system automatically waives all platform fees, ensuring the Buyer receives a 100% refund of 
both their core payment and the convenience fee to maintain absolute trust.

9. Conclusion 
In conclusion, the "HarvestHub" project is designed to provide a practical solution to the real -world 
problems faced by the agricultural community in the North Central Province. By connecting farmers, 
buyers, transporters, and agricultural officers through  a single digital platform, we aim 
to eliminate the middleman and build a transparent "trust ecosystem".  
The system focuses on solving the major issue of crop quality through a manual verification portal. It 
also simplifies logistics by automating transport cost calculations based on weight and distance. By 
following a structured 12 -month work plan and using modern technologies like Spring Boot and 
Android, our team is confident that we can deliver a functional system that benefits boththe industry 
and our technical skills development.
